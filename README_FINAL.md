# From Where She Stood

**Bengali Women Looking at the Colonial World, 1882–1932**

An interactive spatial reading of Krishnabhabini Das and Durgabati Ghose.

This digital object accompanies an M2 Digital Humanities thesis on Bengali women's travel writing, gendered subjectivity, mobility, observation, and colonial modernity.

## About

The project uses spatial close reading to compare meaningful travel episodes across two journeys separated by fifty years. Its central proposition is: **Where she stood changed what she could see, how she was seen, and what she could claim to know.** Rather than treating the map as an exact reconstruction of historical routes, it asks how movement and location shape acts of observation, comparison, self-positioning, and interpretation.

The central descriptive concept is the **travelling gaze**: the traveller's interpretive position changes as she moves through space and encounters people, institutions, infrastructures, and acts of reciprocal observation.

## Digital method

The object combines:
- interactive comparative mapping;
- chronological journey navigation;
- spatial close reading;
- multidimensional comparison by place, gaze, journey stage, observer/observed relation, and historical moment.

Routes are schematic and marker precision varies by episode.

## Primary corpus

- Krishnabhabini Das, *England-e Bangamahila / A Bengali Lady in England*
- Durgabati Ghose, *Paschimjatriki / The Westward Traveller*

The editions used are Krishnabhabini Das, *A Bengali Lady in England*, trans. Somdatta Mandal (Cambridge Scholars Publishing, 2015; original work 1885), and Durgabati Ghose, *The Westward Traveller*, trans. Somdatta Mandal (Orient Blackswan, 2010; original work *Paschimjatriki*, 1936). Full bibliographic references and methodological discussion are provided in the accompanying thesis.

## Technical framework

Built as a static web project using Leaflet for interactive mapping and OpenStreetMap basemap data.
